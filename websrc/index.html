A tiling tree that scrolls.

ymir tiles your windows in columns across an infinitely-scrolling strip — and every new window binary-splits the focused one, Dwindle-style. Open a window and nothing you already have ever resizes. In Rust, on Smithay.

Dwindle: tiling, on the tape

Other scrollable compositors stack flat windows per column. ymir gives each column a recursive binary-split tree — two-dimensional tiling that lives on a one-dimensional scrollable strip.

A tree in every column

New windows split the focused one in two and take half its space. Toggle split orientation, preselect which side the next window takes, and consume, expel, or promote windows through the tree.

→

Dwindle pages

Past a configured windows-per-column cap, a new window starts a fresh full-width Dwindle "page" further along the strip — the tree never sprawls out of control.

fullscreen → still a tile

Fullscreen is just a tile

Fullscreen windows are normal tiles on the strip, not a layer that swallows the desktop. Focus one and it covers the monitor; tab away and it stays fullscreen, ready to return.

Polished in, not bolted on

A custom GL renderer, gestures on every device, and desktop features that generally get shipped as afterthoughts — here they're first-class.

▦ Eye-candy with restraint

Gradient borders in CSS-equivalent color spaces (oklch longer hue, oklab), drop shadows like box-shadow, per-window blur and rounded corners — all VBlank-synced, and stripped from the render tree when disabled.

◨ Live insert hint

Drag a window and a colored focus-ring style hint shows exactly where it'll land — between columns or between tiles.

☾ Lua configuration

Everything lives in ~/.config/ymir/init.lua — includes, live reload, and an imperative ymir.* prelude.

↻ Workspaces that migrate

Dynamic workspaces remember their original output and move straight back when a monitor reconnects — without hijacking your setup.

⌘ Built-in switcher

A recent-windows (Alt-Tab) switcher with live previews, scope filtering, and binds that adapt to your keymap.

◉ Screenshots, native

An area picker with a save-to-disk UI built straight into the compositor — no separate tool required.

▣ Screencasting that thinks

Block out sensitive windows in casts, a dynamic cast target that follows your focus, windowed fullscreen for presentations, and a red border on everything being cast.

⇄ Desktop integration

Systemd and dinit, D-Bus (freedesktop + GNOME/Mutter interfaces), portals, screen lock, and on-demand Xwayland via Xwayland-satellite.

♾ Multi-GPU & a11y

Render-device detection, dmabuf feedback and texture copy between GPUs, plus full screen-reader support via accesskit + AT-SPI.

⚙ IPC for everything

A Unix-socket JSON API with an event stream and a ymir msg CLI to inspect and drive the compositor from anywhere.

⌨ Window & layer rules

Regex-match windows and layer surfaces by app id, title, or namespace to set layout, blur, corner radius, and more.

◔ Final touches

Hot corners, on-screen hotkey overlay, exit-confirm dialog, config-error notifications, and a crossfade screen transition.

Gestures on every device

Not just the touchpad — the mouse, touchscreen, and tablet all get the same fluid, direct-manipulation control.

Mod+drag

Move a window · right-drag to resize · middle-drag to scroll the strip or switch workspaces.

3 fingers

Vertical swipes switch workspaces; horizontal swipes scroll the tiling view.

4 fingers

Swipe up to open the Overview — a zoomed-out look at every workspace.

Drag & drop

Hold over a window to activate it, push an edge to scroll, and drop onto another workspace in the Overview.

Hot corner

Flick the pointer to the top-left corner to flip the Overview on and off.

Overview

Point, click, and drag to move windows between workspaces — a fully mouse-driven workflow.

A philosophy, made concrete

ymir is steered by a handful of explicit principles that are easy to feel when you use it.

01

Opening a window never resizes the ones you already have. The core annoyance of traditional tiling, gone.

02

The focused window never moves on its own. Things happening off the view can't yank what you're working in.

03

Actions apply immediately. Resize and switch instantaneously, even while an animation plays.

04

Eye-candy stays honest. Disabled effects cost nothing; enabled effects don't force needless rendering.

05

Mindful of invisible state. Workspaces remember their original output — but forget it once you work there, so nothing surprising jumps back.
